# Exp02 results: BCM on a single benchmark, scaffold and trace schema (public SWE-bench Pro trajectories)

All numbers come from `results/tables/` (file names in brackets) and are regenerated by `REPRODUCE.md`.
Population: matched tasks only (each task present for every model of the cohort, 14 disputed-label tasks removed).
**One attempt per model per task.** Nothing here measures within-model repeatability.

Primary estimator unless stated: role-aware representation **R2**; the released BCM LightGBM
(`n_estimators=200, lr=0.05, depth 6, 31 leaves`) trained on all cohort trajectories pooled, as in the paper;
out-of-fold **task-grouped** 5-fold CV (`StratifiedGroupKFold` by task, so no task spans train and validation, asserted);
tree-path TreeSHAP on the log-odds margin with additivity asserted per fold (max error observed: 0.0, i.e. <1e-12);
global BCM = mean pairwise cosine over all of a model's trajectories. 95% CIs: paired task-cluster bootstrap
(2,000 resamples, seed 20261026, attributions held fixed, copy-pairs of the same trajectory excluded).
CV-seed variability is reported separately (10 seeds).

## 2. The representation alone reverses the ranking [a2_pairwise, fig1, fig3]

Under the authors' SWE-smith loader (R0), GPT-5 is ranked first with probability 1.00 in the bootstrap,
above every other model by +0.24 to +0.52. Under role-aware parsing (R2) it is ranked last with probability 1.00,
below every model by −0.02 to −0.09 (all CIs exclude 0). The cause is not behavioural: 58% of GPT-5 trajectories
are empty under R0 because GPT-5's thought text is hidden (98% of its action messages have empty content).
Empty rows share one attribution vector per fold, the same mechanism as the 2,513 SWE-smith rows in Exp01.
**The zero-step artefact therefore does not need two data sources. It recurs within one benchmark and one
scaffold, driven by a model property (reasoning visibility).** The same reversal appears in paper-50: R0
ranks gpt-oss first (0.494) and R2 ranks it last (0.016).

Across the 15 representation × predictor configurations, no model keeps its rank (fig3). Kendall τ against the
reference configuration (R2 / released LGBM / seed 0) is 0.8–1.0 across CV seeds within the reference, but
−0.4 to +0.6 across representations and predictor families [a3]. The predictor family changes magnitudes
up to 4× (R2 logistic: Sonnet-4.5 0.50, Haiku 0.46, Gemini 0.42 vs 0.10–0.13 with LGBM). The tuned LGBM
(inner task-grouped CV) agrees with the released LGBM ordering at R2 (τ = 0.8).

Task-grouped vs leave-one-repository-out CV give the same picture (R2 LORO: 0.033 / 0.106 / 0.126 / 0.062 /
0.128 for GPT-5 / Sonnet-4.5 / Haiku / GLM / Gemini).

## 3. Label-permutation negative controls [a4, fig2]

50 pre-specified seeds (1000–1049) per null.

| Primary cohort, R2, released LGBM | real | within-model null median [5%, 95%] | global null median |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-5 | 0.041 | 0.045 [0.021, 0.086] | 0.022 |
| Sonnet-4.5 | 0.107 | 0.116 [0.078, 0.174] | 0.028 |
| Haiku-4.5 | 0.127 | 0.105 [0.066, 0.159] | 0.025 |
| GLM-4.5 | 0.065 | 0.038 [0.016, 0.063] | 0.023 |
| Gemini-2.5-Pro | 0.108 | 0.138 [0.107, 0.201] | 0.031 |

- **Every model's real BCM is inside or at the edge of its within-model null band.** Permuting outcomes
  within each model removes every trajectory–outcome link inside a model and keeps only each model's success
  rate. That alone reproduces the observed BCM level. The same holds with the logistic predictor (R2: 5/5
  inside) and in primary6 (6/6) and primary5_cfgclean. Under the tool-semantic features (R3) the picture is
  mixed: GPT-5 and Gemini are inside, while Haiku (0.210 vs [0.069, 0.147]), GLM (0.093 vs [0.024, 0.084]) and,
  marginally, Sonnet-4.5 (0.159 vs [0.078, 0.155]) exceed their nulls. With semantic features some
  outcome-related within-model structure enters BCM. The effect is small next to the pooling effect (§4).
- The global null (no information at all) gives ≈0.02–0.03. The gap between the global and the within-model
  null is the part of BCM produced by the predictor separating models with different success rates.
- R0: Sonnet-4.5 and Haiku exceed their within-model nulls (p ≤ 0.02), while GPT-5 is below its null median
  (0.55 vs 0.68). Under the flawed loader, high BCM again arises without outcome signal.
- **Exception: Kimi-K2** (paper-50): real 0.505 vs within-model null 0.054 [0.026, 0.100]. See §5.

## 4. BCM magnitude is created by pooling heterogeneous systems [a9, fig6]

The released pipeline fits **one** success predictor on all systems together. We refit it on every subset of
systems containing the target, keeping the target's trajectories and labels fixed.

| Target | alone (k=1) | median, k=2 | k=3 | k=4 | all 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku-4.5 | 0.002 | 0.062 | 0.079 | 0.113 | 0.127 |
| Sonnet-4.5 | 0.004 | 0.048 | 0.073 | 0.095 | 0.107 |
| Gemini-2.5-Pro | 0.001 | 0.047 | 0.074 | 0.095 | 0.108 |
| GLM-4.5 | 0.003 | 0.034 | 0.053 | 0.058 | 0.065 |
| GPT-5 | 0.017 | 0.022 | 0.047 | 0.049 | 0.041 |
| Kimi-K2 (paper-50) | 0.006 | 0.266 | 0.505 | | |

Fit on a single system, **every BCM is ≈0 (0.001–0.019)**, although these single-system predictors are
informative (within-system AUC 0.61–0.77). BCM then rises with the number of co-pooled systems, and a system's
value can change by >0.1 (Haiku) or 0.5 (Kimi) depending only on who else is in the pool. Global BCM, as
operationalised, measures how a system's attributions are offset from the other systems in the pooled
model. It does not measure stability of the system's own success-relevant decision pattern.

## 5. Mechanism: one uniformly signed feature [a8]

High BCM coincides with one feature carrying most of the attribution mass with a uniform sign. Kimi-K2 R2:
`max_action_length` carries 46% of squared attribution and has the same sign in 98.5% of rows (mean +0.81).
Kimi's median longest action is 5,053 characters vs 617–2,133 for the other paper-50 models, so the pooled
model places nearly all Kimi rows on the "long edits → success" side. Within Kimi, success barely varies with
that feature (0.20 → 0.32 across quartiles). GPT-5 under R0: `total_steps` carries 47% with 92% sign agreement
(the empty parses). Models with low BCM spread attribution over several features with mixed signs.

## 6. Task structure dominates the attribution geometry (descriptive) [a6, fig4]

On matched tasks, attribution vectors of **two different models on the same task** are more similar than
one model's vectors across tasks. Under R2, the same-task cross-model cosine is 0.11–0.46 (Sonnet-4.5 vs
Haiku 0.46), while the different-task cosine is −0.09 to 0.11 and within-model global BCM is 0.04–0.13. The
same holds in standardised raw-feature space (same-task 0.11–0.67 vs different-task −0.22 to 0.27). Much of
what global BCM counts as cross-task "inconsistency" is variation that tasks induce in every system.
Caveat: one attempt per model-task, so this does not separate task-induced from stochastic within-model
variation (Exp03 does).

## 7. Confounders of a trajectory's contribution to BCM [a7, a10]

Per-trajectory alignment = cosine to the model's mean attribution direction (global BCM ≈ squared mean
alignment). OLS with model fixed effects and task-clustered SEs (R2): Python tasks −0.161 (SE 0.012) vs Go;
log steps +0.119 (0.008); clean submit +0.097 (0.014); resolved +0.045 (0.011); log reference-patch lines −0.018
(0.007). Adding observable behaviour (ran tests +0.107, reference-file recall +0.101, extra files +0.037;
`a10`) raises R² from 0.032 (model only) to 0.073. BCM contributions depend on task language and trajectory
length, and only weakly reflect observable strategy.

## 8. BCM vs predictor quality [a5, fig5]

Across all model × representation × predictor configurations, BCM is weakly *negatively* related to the
predictor's within-model AUC (Spearman −0.11 released LGBM, −0.16 logistic, −0.50 tuned LGBM; real labels).
Null models with chance-level within-model AUC (≈0.50) reach mean BCMs up to ≈0.87 (R0 logistic, GPT-5). A high BCM does
not indicate that the explained predictor captures success-relevant behaviour.

## 9. Agreement with Exp01 and the original paper

| Claim | Original paper | Exp01 (confounded cohort) | Exp02 (matched, single scaffold) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proprietary ≫ open BCM | 0.77–0.83 vs 0.07–0.09 | separation shrinks 4× with role-aware parse | **not supported**: R2 BCM 0.04–0.13 for all, open GLM-4.5 (0.065) between GPT-5 (0.041) and the Claudes; paper-50 open Kimi highest (0.505), open gpt-oss lowest (0.016) |
| BCM reflects consistency of success-relevant behaviour | claimed | real ≤ within-system null | **not supported**: real ≈ within-model null; ≈0 when fit per system |
| Parser artefact | — | empty parses inflate SWE-smith systems | **replicated within one benchmark**: GPT-5 58% empty under the authors' loader → rank 1 → rank 5 |
| Within-task > between-task | yes | yes (all systems once parsing is fixed) | cross-model same-task similarity > within-model cross-task (descriptive) |
| Rankings stable to folds | — | yes | yes within a configuration (τ ≥ 0.8); not across representation/predictor |

## Negative and null results (kept)
- Under R2 (primary representation) no model's BCM exceeds its within-model null except Kimi-K2, which a cohort-relative length feature explains. Under R3, three primary models modestly exceed their nulls (positive result for BCM, kept).
- Tuning the predictor did not change conclusions. LORO did not change conclusions.
- Bootstrap CIs are narrow because they hold attributions fixed. Pairwise differences "significant" under the bootstrap are stable properties of the pipeline, not evidence of behavioural differences (see §3–4).
